The open question: where the stat row goes

Full width (default)

Hero and the four stats form a header band across both columns, then the cards split below. The numbers get a proper row instead of a cramped 2×2, and both columns start at the same line. Cost: on a settled period the rail holds only Spiffs and the RO list, so it can run short while the left column runs long.

In rail

Stats sit at the top of the right column as a 2×2, keeping the rail full on every period. Cost: four numbers in a narrow column, the hero is left alone spanning only part of the width, and the two columns start on different footings.

Rules this mockup is demonstrating

  • Soft modes. Mode changes emphasis, order, and what's expanded — never what exists. Demoted content sits under the “Reference” divider, which becomes the right rail in two-column.
  • Nine peers → two families. Discrepancy, Reconciliation and Dispute collapse into “Did I get paid?” as one progressive drill-down. Unpaid Time and Pay Check-Up merge into “What did the work cost me?” — they already share a computation in code.
  • Scope decides the surface. If a number needs data from outside this period, it isn't on this page. Trends go to /insights; in-the-moment prompts go to the timer.
  • Degrade out loud. A card that can't compute says what's missing and links the fix. Two live examples above — one in “In progress”, one in “Settled”.
  • The period is the page title. The old “Pay Period” heading and the whole picker card are gone; ‹ › steps through periods, ⋮ holds custom dates and reset.
  • RO list follows the mode. Fully expanded mid-period when it's your working log; capped with “Show all 58” once the period is settled and Reconciliation is the real drill-down.

Deliberately not fixed here

  • Discrepancy (0.1 period-level tolerance) and Reconciliation (0.05 per-line) can disagree. Merging them makes it visible — the fix is a product decision, not a layout one.
  • /insights has to exist before Warranty mix-shift (11.5) and comeback-rate analytics (11.9) have anywhere to land.
  • The in-progress hero's projection overlaps the dashboard's pace bar and forecast line. Logged, not resolved.
  • The “recovered all-time” figure comes out of the dispute card — the dashboard already owns it.

Fidelity notes

  • Colours, radii, shadows and semantic tokens are lifted verbatim from src/app/globals.css, both themes.
  • Type is a system stack — IBM Plex Sans and JetBrains Mono can't load here, so exact metrics will shift slightly in the real build.
  • Numbers are representative, not from your database. The two closed-period modes describe the same period so you can compare them directly.
